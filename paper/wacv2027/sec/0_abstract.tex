\begin{abstract}
Reasoning-based driving vision-language-action (VLA) models propose multiple candidate trajectories at each decision, yet only one is executed.
Standard evaluation scores the best candidate, assuming an oracle selector that deployment lacks, and leaves the executed candidate's safety cost unaudited.
Existing online evaluators close this gap with explicit imagination, rolling out each candidate through a purpose-trained world model at inference time.
We introduce \headname{}, a pre-execution auditor that reuses the consequence knowledge a frozen driving VLA computes while proposing its candidates.
\headname{} reads the last hidden token of the VLA's prompt prefill and scores each candidate with a lightweight action-conditioned consequence head; consequence prediction supervises training only, so the audit adds neither rollouts nor VLA training.
We tie the reused representation bit-exactly to the candidate-generating forward pass and evaluate all claims under a preregistered protocol on scenes unseen by any model in the study.
In closed-loop simulation with a 10B VLA, \headname{} recovers a scene-specific safety signal that a capacity-matched scene-blind auditor cannot, and that disappears when the representation is swapped or zeroed.
Notably, reading the entire prefill sequence underperforms reading its last token alone.
An adequately powered comparison with a trained trajectory-geometry auditor shows that the reused representation adds information yet does not by itself displace it.
These results indicate that pre-execution safety auditing can reuse the generator's own forward pass rather than be delegated to online imagination.
\end{abstract}

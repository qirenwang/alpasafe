\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reasoning-based vision-language-action (VLA) models are becoming the planning core of modern autonomous-driving stacks~\cite{tian2024drivevlm,hwang2024emma,alpamayo2025}.
Given a scene and a driving prompt, such a model proposes several candidate trajectories, and the vehicle executes one of them.
The field's dominant reporting convention, minADE@$K$ and its closed-loop analogues, scores the best of the $K$ candidates and thereby assumes an oracle selector that deployment does not have.
What the vehicle actually experiences is decided by the candidate it commits to; the difference between the best available candidate and the executed one is the selected regret, and for a safety-critical system it is precisely this pre-execution decision that deserves an audit.
In practice, the critical question is not whether the generator could have driven safely, but whether the trajectory it is about to execute will.

Existing online trajectory evaluators answer this question with explicit imagination.
WoTE~\cite{li2025wote} trains a BEV world model alongside the planner, autoregressively imagines each candidate's future state, and scores the imagined futures with imitation and simulation-derived rewards.
This design falls short in two key respects for a pre-execution audit of a large driving VLA.
First, it requires a purpose-trained world model and per-candidate rollouts at inference time, a cost that grows with the number of candidates and competes with the generator for the same latency budget.
Second, it treats the generator as a black box: the imagination is computed from scratch, even though a VLA trained to produce good trajectories must already represent internally where hazards are, which maneuvers are viable, and how the scene constrains them.
To bridge this gap, we ask whether the consequence knowledge a safety audit needs is already present in the generator's own forward pass, in a form that a small head can read without any rollout.

To this end, we introduce \headname{}, a pre-execution safety auditor for reasoning-based driving VLAs (\cref{fig:arch}).
\headname{} captures the final-layer hidden state of the frozen VLA's prompt prefill during the same forward pass that generates the candidates, and reads only its last token, a byproduct of generation that costs nothing to obtain.
A lightweight action-conditioned consequence head then scores each candidate independently: it embeds the candidate trajectory, queries the scene representation through a single cross-attention block, and predicts the candidate's closed-loop official score, decomposed into a group-level difficulty term and a bounded per-candidate residual so that only the latter drives the audit.
Consequence heads for future ego motion, progress, collision, and off-road events supervise the representation during training but are deleted at deployment; the audited score is bit-identical without them.
Consequences thus shape the auditor's weights during training, but nothing is simulated or predicted at decision time.

Because the effects at stake are small relative to a strong generator, we treat the evaluation design as part of the contribution.
The reused representation is tied to the candidate-generating forward pass by capture-time hashes, so representation and candidates are provably co-produced.
Every confirmatory study is preregistered with frozen decision gates before outcomes exist, evaluated on scenes no model in the study had consumed, and analyzed with paired scene bootstrap intervals; the final comparison against a trained geometric auditor is sized in advance to detect the pilot effect.
All numbers reported in this paper are read directly from hash-locked analysis records.

We validate \headname{} on a 10B driving VLA in closed-loop simulation.
Our analysis shows that the frozen last token carries a scene-specific audit signal: on prospectively frozen, never-before-touched scenes, \headname{} lowers selected regret and raises top-1 agreement over a scene-blind auditor of identical architecture and capacity, and the gain disappears when the representation is swapped for another scene's or zeroed.
Notably, giving the same auditor the full 3{,}086-token prefill sequence makes it worse than reading the last token alone, under a matched parameter budget.
Against a trained trajectory-geometry auditor, an adequately powered comparison on 525 scenes shows that the reused representation adds information but does not by itself displace the tuned geometric baseline, a boundary result that delimits when representation reuse pays.
The deployed auditor has 1.7M parameters and scores all candidates in about one millisecond.
Our contributions are three-fold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce pre-execution safety auditing of driving VLAs as a frozen-representation readout problem, and present \headname{}, an action-conditioned consequence auditor that reads the frozen generator's own prefill state with zero VLA training and zero inference-time rollouts.
  \item We present a same-prefill capture protocol that ties the reused representation bit-exactly to the candidate-generating forward pass, together with a preregistered, prospectively replicated evaluation design that we argue should become standard for auditor claims.
  \item We show that the last prefill token carries a causally verified, scene-specific audit signal, that the full sequence is worse than its last token under matched capacity, and, at adequate statistical power, where representation reuse does and does not displace a tuned geometric auditor.
\end{itemize}

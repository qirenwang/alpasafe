\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\headname{} is a pre-execution auditor for reasoning-based driving VLAs that reads the consequence knowledge already present in the frozen generator's forward pass.
As shown in \cref{fig:arch}, one forward pass of the VLA yields both the $K$ candidate trajectories and the prefill hidden states; \headname{} captures the last token of those states, pairs it with each candidate, and scores the candidate with a lightweight action-conditioned consequence head whose auxiliary consequence predictions are used only during training.
This section describes the audited quantity (\cref{sec:setting}), the same-prefill capture that ties the representation to the candidates (\cref{sec:capture}), the auditor head (\cref{sec:head}), its training objectives (\cref{sec:objectives}), and the frozen protocol under which every claim is evaluated (\cref{sec:protocol}).

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

We work in \simname, an internal high-fidelity closed-loop driving simulator whose official scene score $y\in[0,1]$ aggregates safety, compliance, and progress into a single scalar per rollout.
The official score is the sole audit supervision: submetric aggregates are deliberately barred from the auditor's objective so that it cannot game individual submetrics, and consequence submetrics enter only as auxiliary heads (\cref{sec:head}).
Each scene contributes three decision groups at staggered decision times.
For each group, the frozen generator \vlaname{} (10B), a reasoning VLM coupled to a trajectory decoder (\cref{fig:arch}a), samples $K{=}8$ candidate trajectories from one prompt prefill; every candidate is then executed in closed loop and receives its own official score, giving 24 scoring rollouts per scene.

Given a group $g$ with candidates $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_K$ and scores $y(\tau_k)$, an auditor $\pi$ commits to $\hat\tau=\pi(g)$.
We audit two co-primary quantities:
selected regret, $\regret(g) = \max_k y(\tau_k) - y(\hat\tau)$ (lower is better; the oracle gap actually paid at deployment), and
tie-aware top-1 agreement, $\mathbf{1}[\,y(\hat\tau) \ge \max_k y(\tau_k) - \epsilon\,]$ with $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ (higher is better).
Both aggregate group $\rightarrow$ scene mean $\rightarrow$ seed mean $\rightarrow$ paired scene bootstrap (\cref{sec:protocol}).

\subsection{Same-Prefill Capture}
\label{sec:capture}

A representation-reuse claim is confounded if the representation could come from a different forward pass than the candidates, for example a different context window, different preprocessing, or a silently re-run prefill.
We eliminate this by construction.
A capture hook inside the production \texttt{predict()} path records the final transformer layer's hidden states (post final normalization) of the prompt prefill, $H\in\mathbb{R}^{3086\times4096}$ (the prompt length is invariant across groups), during the same call that samples the $K$ candidates (\cref{fig:capture}), with an exactly-one-prefill assertion so that a second prefill in the call fails the capture rather than silently substituting context.
The scene vector used at deployment is the last row, $h = H[-1]\in\mathbb{R}^{4096}$.
Capture-time SHA-256 hashes of both tensors establish bit-exact provenance ($h \equiv H[-1]$ verified for every group), and every downstream artifact, from training tables to locked predictions and analysis records, carries these hashes forward.
Candidates, representation, and scores are therefore certifiably co-produced; the ablations in \cref{sec:res-signal} then test whether the representation's content matters.

\subsection{Action-Conditioned Consequence Auditor}
\label{sec:head}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/AlpaSafe.png}
  \caption{\textbf{\headname{} architecture.} (a) One forward pass of the frozen \vlaname{} generator: its reasoning VLM consumes the camera views and the driving prompt, its trajectory decoder samples the $K{=}8$ candidates $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_K$, and the last hidden token $h$ of the VLM's prompt prefill is captured in the same pass as a free byproduct. (b) The \headname{} auditor (1.7M parameters, the only trained component) scores each candidate independently with shared weights: a scene reader embeds $h$ and feeds both a group-mean head and a scene-trajectory fusion block (a single cross-attention layer) that combines it with the trajectory encoder's embedding of $\tau_i$; the audited score $S_i=\hat y(\tau_i)$ sums the group-mean head $\hat m_g$ (shared by all candidates of a group, so it cannot affect the choice) and the bounded residual head $\hat r$ (\cref{eq:decomp}); the best-scored candidate is executed. Auxiliary consequence heads are training-only and deleted at inference; the deployed score is bit-identical. Scores and trajectories shown are illustrative.}
  \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

The \headname{} head (\cref{fig:arch}; 1{,}708{,}871 trainable parameters; the frozen VLA receives no gradients) scores each candidate independently.
There is no cross-candidate attention, so scores are $K$-invariant and permutation-equivariant by construction; we verify that off-diagonal cross-candidate gradients are exactly zero and that scores are bit-identical whether a candidate is scored alone or in a batch.
A trajectory encoder embeds the candidate; a scene-trajectory fusion block, a single cross-attention layer, queries the scene representation (the last token $h$, or in ablation arms the full sequence $H$); shared trunk features feed the output heads.

Naively regressing $y$ conflates two signals of very different scale: the group's overall difficulty and the candidate's relative quality within the group, and only the latter matters for the audit.
Our design-phase studies showed the conflation concretely: an unconstrained residual develops gradient conflict between ranking and centered-regression losses, and group-level offsets dominate the representation.
We therefore decompose
\begin{equation}
  \hat y(\tau) \;=\; \hat m_g \;+\; S\,\tanh\!\big(\hat r(\tau)/S\big),
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\hat m_g$ is a group-mean head, $\hat r(\tau)$ is a per-candidate residual (zero-initialized), and the bound $S{=}0.67$, frozen from development-cohort residual statistics, keeps the residual inside the empirically observed spread.
The deployed audit commits to $\hat\tau=\arg\max_\tau \hat r(\tau)$: the group-mean head is excluded by construction, so scene difficulty cannot leak into the choice.

Auxiliary heads predict per-candidate consequences: future ego trajectory ($64\times2$), progress, collision, and off-road events.
They are training-only.
At inference the deployed score is bit-identical with the auxiliary heads deleted, which we verify with a deletion test in the conformance suite.
Consequences shape the representation during training but are never simulated, predicted, or consumed at decision time, in contrast to explicit-imagination designs~\cite{li2025wote}.

\subsection{Training Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

All representation arms share one architecture and parameter count (state dicts are interchangeable); arms differ only in scene-input visibility and objective.
The checkpoint objective, used by all arms for model selection during training, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  L \;=\; & 1.0\,L_{\text{rank}} + 0.5\,L_{\text{centered}} + 0.1\,L_{\text{group-mean}} \\
  & + 0.25\,L_{\text{future}} + 0.1\,L_{\text{progress}} \\
  & + 0.1\,L_{\text{collision}} + 0.1\,L_{\text{offroad}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The first three terms supervise the decomposed score of \cref{eq:decomp}: a weighted pairwise softplus ranking loss and a centered regression on the residual $\hat r$, and a regression on the group mean $\hat m_g$.
The last four supervise the auxiliary consequence heads of \cref{sec:head}; all regressions are scale-matched SmoothL1.

Ranking all $K$ candidates well is not the deployed task; putting the best candidate on top is.
The selector objective replaces the ranking term of \cref{eq:objective}, whose coefficient moves to 0, with a regret-weighted best-vs-rest logistic loss entering at coefficient $0.026$.
With tied-best set $B_g=\{i: y_{\max}-y_i\le\epsilon\}$ and per-candidate selector scores $s$,
\begin{equation}
  L_{\text{sel}} \;=\; \frac{1}{W}\sum_{g}\frac{1}{|B_g|}\sum_{i\in B_g}\sum_{j\notin B_g} w_j\,
  \mathrm{softplus}\!\big(\tfrac{s_j-s_i}{\tau}\big),
  \label{eq:selloss}
\end{equation}
where $w_j = y_{\max}-y_j$ is the regret of candidate $j$ and $W=\sum_g \sum_{j\notin B_g} w_j$ is its total mass.
Each pair of a tied-best candidate $i$ and a non-best candidate $j$ thus pays a logistic penalty for placing $s_j$ near or above $s_i$, weighted by exactly the regret that executing $j$ would cost; the inner mean spreads the target over ties, the normalization by $W$ makes $L_{\text{sel}}$ a regret-weighted average of these penalties, and all-tie groups ($B_g$ covering all $K$ candidates) contribute no pairs.
Both scalars are resolved mechanically from frozen target metadata rather than tuned: $\tau=0.043$ is the median positive oracle-best-to-candidate score gap (ceiled to 0.001), and $0.026$ equalizes the RMS per-candidate gradient $\partial L/\partial s$ against the replaced ranking term at equal scores.

The natural alternative, minimizing the expected regret of a softmax policy, $L_A=\mathbb{E}_{q}[r]$ with $q=\mathrm{softmax}(s/\tau)$, was rejected on a closed, preregistered criterion: its gradient $\partial L_A/\partial s_j = \tfrac{1}{\tau}q_j(r_j-\mathbb{E}_q[r])$ vanishes as $q$ collapses onto a single candidate, that is, exactly when the auditor is confidently wrong, the one failure mode the objective exists to repair.
The best-vs-rest logistic instead saturates at its maximum there.
In the operating regime our development runs actually produce (within-group score spreads routinely $2.6$--$10\times$ $\tau$), the softmax gradient retains as little as $3.9\%$ of its small-spread magnitude; reproduced synthetically in the preregistration conformance suite, the softmax gradient norm collapses to $1.3\times 10^{-60}$ while the selector loss holds $24.9$.
This geometry, not any real-model outcome comparison, fixed the objective before the confirmatory studies ran.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Every confirmatory study follows one frozen protocol.
Training and evaluation use 5-fold cross-validation split by scene with three seeds; checkpoint selection uses inner validation only, and the test fold is never consulted.
All per-candidate predictions for every arm and every ablation condition are hash-locked before any endpoint is computed; the analyzer verifies every hash and its own code hash against the registered one, and runs exactly once.
Statistics use a paired scene percentile bootstrap with $10{,}000$ resamples and a frozen seed; undefined scenes are dropped and counted, never zero-filled; we compute 95\% confidence intervals (CIs) throughout and treat an effect as supported only when its CI excludes zero; CIs are tabulated in \cref{tab:main}, and the text quotes point estimates together with whether the CI excludes zero.
Arms, endpoints, gates, margins, ablation mappings, and analysis code are frozen and hash-registered before outcomes exist; deviations would be visible as hash breaks.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Driving scenes often admit several valid maneuvers, motivating candidate-based formulations that preserve multiple trajectory hypotheses rather than regress a single averaged future~\cite{chai2019multipath,ettinger2021waymo}.
Such planners commonly encode the observation into a shared scene representation and use it to generate or score candidate trajectories.
UniAD and VAD employ BEV or vectorized scene features for prediction and planning, while VADv2 explicitly models a distribution over possible ego plans~\cite{hu2023uniad,jiang2023vad,chen2024vadv2}.
Hydra-MDP, GTRS, and DriveSuprim further separate candidate generation from scene-conditioned scoring, whereas DrivoR compresses multi-camera features into learned register tokens shared by its trajectory-generation and scoring decoders~\cite{li2024hydramdp,li2025gtrs,yao2026drivesuprim,kirby2026drivor}.
Driving foundation models similarly integrate internal visual-language representations with trajectory generation, with stochastic or generative decoders supporting multiple possible plans~\cite{shao2024lmdrive,tian2024drivevlm,hwang2024emma,alpamayo2025}.
Recent studies also consume these representations downstream: BLUE uses a frozen VLA hidden state for language-routing decisions, Planning Token examines how planning information develops across decoder layers, and LWDrive uses world-model-supervised layerwise features to refine and score trajectory candidates~\cite{ling2026blue,babu2026planningtoken,yang2026lwdrive}.
Together, these works show that internal scene representations are central to multimodal generation and selection; their main differences lie in whether the representation is purpose-trained with the planner, adapted using additional supervision, or reused from an unchanged generator.

Trajectory evaluators differ in whether they predict consequences before assigning value.
PDM rolls out kinematic proposals against simplified agent forecasts and applies hand-designed rules~\cite{dauner2023pdm}.
Direct learned approaches instead build evaluator knowledge into a scorer during training: Hydra-MDP distills rule-based teachers, while GTRS, DriveSuprim, DrivoR, and CLOVER predict trajectory values or metric sub-scores without generating future scenes at inference time~\cite{li2024hydramdp,li2025gtrs,yao2026drivesuprim,kirby2026drivor,ang2026clover}.
TOAD further treats a learned scorer as a reward for test-time trajectory optimization~\cite{xu2026toad}.
These approaches reduce runtime modeling cost, but their scores implicitly encode the dynamics and evaluator used during training.

Explicit-imagination methods instead condition a world model on each candidate before evaluation.
WoTE predicts candidate-conditioned future BEV states, whereas SafeDrive constructs sparse future worlds containing safety-critical agents and road elements~\cite{li2025wote,kim2026safedrive}.
WPT transfers world-model guidance into a lightweight policy so that the world model can be removed at deployment~\cite{jiang2026wpt}.
Related foundation-model approaches use a separate generative VLM as a trajectory reward model or invoke a VLA critic to evaluate and refine an initial plan~\cite{chen2026drivereward,yang2026criticvla}.
Together, these methods span a continuum from explicit online prediction to scoring with no inference-time prediction at all, trading consequence-level interpretability against inference cost and dependence on a learned dynamics model.

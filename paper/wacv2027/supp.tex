\section{Selector-loss resolution}
\label{supp:selloss}

Two candidate top-of-list objectives were resolved by a preregistered 12-criterion analysis with \emph{no} real-model outcome comparison permitted.

\paragraph{Candidate A (rejected): expected regret of a softmax policy.}
$L_A=\sum_j q_j r_j$, $q=\mathrm{softmax}(s/\tau)$, $r_j = y_{\max}-y_j$.
Exact in the $\tau\to0$ limit, but
$\partial L_A/\partial s_j = \tfrac{1}{\tau}\,q_j\,(r_j - \textstyle\sum_k q_k r_k)$:
as $q$ collapses onto any single candidate---including a \emph{wrong} one---every component tends to zero.
The gradient vanishes exactly in the failure mode the objective exists to repair (a confidently wrong selector).
In the residual-spread regime the development runs actually produce (within-group spread median $0.112$, i.e.\ spread$/\tau$ of $2.6$--$10.1$, up to $31$ at the residual bound $2S$), candidate A retains as little as $3.9\%$ of its small-spread gradient magnitude.

\paragraph{Candidate B (selected): regret-weighted best-vs-rest logistic (Eq.~(2) of the main paper).}
Bounded per-candidate gradient ($|\partial L/\partial s|\le 1/(2\tau)$ after normalization) that \emph{saturates at its maximum} when the selector is confidently wrong; simultaneously a surrogate for the co-primary top-1 endpoint, since the argmax is correct iff every best-vs-rest margin is positive, each weighted by the regret that mistake would cost.
Tied-best candidates form no pairs among themselves, so no arbitrary tie-breaking enters the loss.

\paragraph{Mechanical scalars.}
$\tau=\SelTau$: the 0.001-ceiled median positive oracle-best-to-candidate official-score gap over frozen training targets (raw value $0.0430$).
Coefficient $=\SelCoef$: matches the RMS per-candidate gradient $\partial L/\partial s$ of the replaced ranking term at equal selector scores (measured ratio stable across batches, $0.024$--$0.045$); the shared-trunk gradient could not define this coefficient because the residual head is zero-initialized, making the trunk ratio $0/0$ at initialization.
The replacement is a replacement, not an addition: both losses share the $w\cdot\mathrm{softplus}(-\text{margin}/\text{scale})$ family and pooling, so a single scalar commensurates them; sweeps, annealing, and outcome-based tuning were contractually forbidden.
The preregistration conformance suite reproduces the decisive geometry synthetically: at a confidently-wrong operating point the rejected objective's gradient norm is \SelGradVanish{} while the frozen objective holds \SelGradSat.

\section{Negative-results timeline}
\label{supp:negatives}

The path to the last-token result consumed several attractive hypotheses; we record them because each closed a design branch other groups may be tempted to reopen.
\todo{re-pull the exact figures below from the registered stage records for camera-ready; flagged values are from stage summaries}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Compressed sequence summaries fail.} A learned compression of the prefill sequence showed \emph{negative} content value: zeroing the compressed features \emph{helped} (content contribution $\approx-0.032$), refuting the compressed-representation route with CI support and redirecting the program to the raw states.
  \item \textbf{The pooled vector is mostly a group-level offset.} $98.2\%$ of the pooled representation's linear effect on scores was a per-group offset, not candidate discrimination---motivating the decomposed head (Eq.~(1) of the main paper), which routes group-level information to $\hat m_g$ and excludes it from selection.
  \item \textbf{Rank/centered gradient conflict.} Unbounded residuals produced cosine conflict ($-0.22$ to $-0.41$) between ranking and centered-regression gradients; the bounded residual repaired alignment ($+0.02$ to $+0.34$, spread $-48\%$) while remaining endpoint-neutral---a mechanism repair, not a headline gain, retained for its side effects on trainability.
  \item \textbf{Replay is not bit-exact.} Recomputing representations by replaying stored inputs diverged at one bf16 ULP in a minority of anchors---enough to break provenance. This killed every retroactive-capture design and forced the prospective same-prefill capture contract: representations are stored at generation time or not used at all.
  \item \textbf{Retroactive recovery is impossible in practice.} For an earlier 366-group cohort, full input closure (frame indices, ego poses, verification hashes) could not be reconstructed from any surviving artifact---sweeping 1{,}068 stored logs recovered none---which is why the cohort ladder persists input closure from the start.
\end{itemize}
